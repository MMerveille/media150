Reading 3: Motherhood in Crisis: My Stolen Future

My Reflection

After reading Motherhood in Crisis: My Stolen Future, I was deeply affected by the story. Women, all around the world, in fact throughout all of history have been abused, discriminated against, and treated horribly. The illustration communicated that beautifully, because instead of simply presenting information like a textbook, or just showing a moment in a photograph, the illustrations felt like you were walking through the moment. Like you were a ghost beside Heela, watching and unable to do anything. Only observe. The illustrations encouraged me to look beyond the facts, and simply dig into the emotions and trauma Heela faced.

Visually, I was impressed by the artwork. The usage of colors can influence how we interpret a scene, and by doing so creates an atmosphere that feels like what the scene is trying to convey. The characters' expressions too, to me, were able to spark anger in me. Her face when the sexual assault happened, when she found out she was pregnant and just came to terms with it, was able to spark emotion in me. The composition of each image also helps guide the reader's attention, and I was really interested in the portrayal of Patrick, Heela's rapist. The shading, and the fully blacked out drawing of him showing his cowardice and unforgivable sin was perfect. Instead of relying on words alone to tell us who a person is, the artist can use color, shading, and other artistic techniques to convey the scene better than sometimes a photo can.

Speaking of photographs, photographs capture a moment that existed in front of a camera, preserving a moment in time. An illustration, however, involves you actually constructing an image through deliberate artistic techniques and decisions. The illustrator chooses how to portray a person, where to work, and what to emphasize. Combine that with the practice of getting it right, the mistakes made, and then the finished final product, and the image starts to maybe express more than a photograph because of the work being put in. Compared with text alone, illustrations provide an additional layer of communication, and often in better form. Personally, I get put to sleep when shown long lists of text. Pictures to me keep me engaged and keep me engaged. When text and illustrations work together however, the reader can process the story both intellectually and emotionally. The artwork also gives the story a sense of movement and order, like you're watching a film. This can allow different subjects to be engaging without diminishing their original purpose.

Ultimately, what made this story memorable to me was the way its illustrations gave emotional weight to Heela's experiences. I felt for Heela so much, along with the others and their stories. They shouldn't have had to go through something like that, and it's a privilege, however many others want to take it away, that live in a country that protects and provides for women. And the art shown today was not simply about making something visually appealing; but about channeling emotion. Photography and writing remain powerful storytelling tools, but illustration offers its own different form of expression. By creating images rather than simply recording existing moments, artists can encourage us to feel, reflect, and connect with stories that might otherwise remain distant from us.
