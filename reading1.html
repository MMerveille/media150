Reading 1: On Photography

Marcky Merveille

After reading Susan Sontag's essay, I found that photography and image-making aren't simply about taking pictures. Pictures and images can tell us stories that evoke emotions in us, such as solitude, depression, triumph, defeat, togetherness, and brutality. Sontag, like me, also aligns with the notion that photographs give us a feeling of "possessing or understanding the world," but not understanding it fully because of our limited perspectives. That's exactly why we keep telling stories: to bridge the gap, as much as we can, between our perspectives and the world.

For me, I use photography for the mundane and the beautiful. I try to capture people, places, and emotions that speak to me. Whether that's a sunrise, sunset, a rainy day, people walking about, someone arguing, or just a cat minding its own business. I love taking pictures because it grounds me in the daily life that I live in and makes me gush about how beautiful or drastic something is, ought to be, or can be.

This sort of connects to Sontag's "Ethics of Seeing," where by taking a photo of someone, you violate them by seeing them in a light where they don't see themselves. I agree because not everyone will agree with the message or story behind a photo. A person might not agree that they look pristine in a photo, thus, in that case, "violating" their sense of self.

Ultimately, I think photography allows me to understand the world in the ways only I know. I try to represent the world through ordinary moments, whether that's beauty, ugliness, connection, loneliness, peaceful moments, or chaotic ones. That is what the world and photography mean to me: every image tells a story, but every story is shaped by the person who's holding the camera.
