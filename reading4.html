Reading 4: Sara Stern's Presentation

My Reflection

Sara Stern’s presentation was a really interesting experience for me because her work was so unique and engaging. One of her specialties, stop motion, stood out to me most. I found her stop-motion puppets to be super quirky, expressive, and full of personality. Her singing on top of the animation and her odd but funny narration made the experience all the more brilliant. It reminded me that art is really whatever you wanted it to be, and that conventional ways aren't ways to capture someone's attention. Sometimes, the quirky and weird can be just as beautiful as the sophisticated and dignified.

Another part of Sara’s presentation that I appreciated was her discussion of queer art and her introduction to Barbara Hammer, a lesbian filmmaker and artist. I thought it was interesting that Sara took the time to share Hammer’s work and history and discuss her contributions, and how it influenced her, a queer woman herself, to make the art she wants to make. Her presentation showed me how art can be more than a visual experience; it can also be a way for people to express their identities and explore their experiences. Because she was queer, knowing that she shares a personal connection with the topic made her discussion feel more relatable and genuine. It was nice to listen to someone speak about a topic they really enjoy, but also something that could also connect to their own experiences. That kind of personal connection can make a presentation more impactful because it allows the audience to understand the subject from a perspective they might not have considered before.

In conclusion, Sara Stern’s presentation reminded me that art can take many different forms and can be communicated in many different ways, while still being entertaining. Her stop-motion puppets caught my attention because of their quirky nature, and her discussion of queer artists gave me a lot to think about. I liked the way she presented work through her interests, her personality, and the way she lives her life. On top of that, she gave really good, credible advice on her work, how she deals with creative blocks, and how to deal with everyday human struggles like procrastination. She wasn’t just an artist who was out of reach, but an artist almost everybody can relate to, which made the time she spent with us all the more genuine.
